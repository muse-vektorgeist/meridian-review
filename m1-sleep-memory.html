Why Sleep Is a Skill

By Priya Raman · 6 min read · September 2026

We treat sleep like a switch — something that should flip off on command at 11pm. Sleep researchers will tell you it's closer to a skill: a trainable sequence of wind-down behaviors that teaches your nervous system the day is over.

The core protocol is unglamorous. Same bedtime, same wake time, weekends included. Dim the lights an hour before bed; the drop in light is the primary signal your brain uses to start melatonin production. Keep the bedroom cool — around 65°F — because core body temperature needs to fall for sleep onset, and a warm room fights that drop all night.

"You don't fall asleep. You practice the conditions, and sleep arrives on its own schedule."

Caffeine has a half-life of five to six hours, which means the 3pm coffee is still a quarter present at midnight. Alcohol is worse: it sedates you into unconsciousness but fragments the second half of the night, suppressing the REM sleep where emotional processing happens. The people who "sleep fine after wine" are usually the ones waking at 3am wondering why.

None of this works in a single night. Like any skill, sleep responds to reps — two to three weeks of consistent routine before the system recalibrates. The payoff compounds quietly: better mornings first, then better afternoons, then one day you realize you haven't thought about being tired in weeks.
